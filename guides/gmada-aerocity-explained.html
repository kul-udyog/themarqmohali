GMADA Aerocity Explained: The Master Plan Behind PR-7 Airport Road

If you're evaluating a home on Airport Road, Sector 82, you've run into the name "GMADA Aerocity" more than once. Here's what it actually is, how the zoning works, and what it means for a residential purchase in this corridor — sourced from the same GMADA-approved site plan and zoning documents used to plan The Marq by Atlantis.

What is GMADA?

GMADA — the Greater Mohali Area Development Authority — is the state planning authority responsible for zoning, infrastructure approval, and land development across Mohali (SAS Nagar) and the surrounding urban area. Any residential or commercial project in this belt, including Aerocity, is developed under a GMADA-approved zoning plan before construction can begin.

What is Aerocity?

Aerocity is a GMADA-planned district running along the Chandigarh airport corridor, covering Sector 82 and the sectors around it. It's zoned for mixed use — residential, commercial, and multiplex/entertainment development — rather than as a single-purpose residential colony, which is why projects here typically combine apartment towers with street-level retail (SCO plots) rather than standing alone.

The Marq by Atlantis sits within this district, at PR-7 Airport Road, Block-B, GMADA Aerocity, Sector 82 — a plot zoned under this same mixed-use framework, which is why the development pairs four residential towers with an internal SCO retail street.

How the zoning actually works

Plots in Aerocity are approved against Punjab's Urban Planning and Development Building Rules, 2021, which set out ground coverage limits, Floor Area Ratio (FAR), parking ratios, and setback requirements before GMADA signs off on a site plan. A few provisions are worth knowing as a buyer:

  • Mixed-use, not single-use. Plots along this corridor are zoned for residential, commercial, and multiplex use together — not residential alone — which is why you'll see retail frontage built into most Aerocity towers.
  • Height is airport-cleared, not just GMADA-cleared. Because the corridor sits within the Chandigarh airport's approach path, building heights need a No-Objection Certificate from the Airport Authority of India / Indian Air Force, in addition to standard municipal building norms — an extra layer of regulatory sign-off specific to this stretch of road.
  • Green-building provisions are mandatory, not optional. Electric-vehicle charging infrastructure and rooftop rainwater harvesting are required under current Punjab building policy for new developments of this scale — not a developer add-on to market, but a baseline compliance requirement.
  • Parking is formula-driven. Group housing and commercial parking are calculated against Floor Area Ratio under the 2021 building rules, rather than a fixed per-unit number — which is part of why double basement parking is standard for towers of this size.
None of this is marketing copy — it's drawn from the same GMADA-approved zoning and site-plan documentation used to plan The Marq by Atlantis. If you're comparing multiple Aerocity projects, asking to see the approved zoning plan and RERA registration for each one is the fastest way to verify claims made on any project's website, including this one.

Frequently asked

Is Aerocity Mohali the same as Sector 82?
Aerocity is a GMADA-planned zone that includes Sector 82 and neighbouring sectors along the airport corridor — Sector 82 is a location within Aerocity, not a separate area.

Why does building height near Aerocity require airport clearance?
Sites on this corridor fall within the Chandigarh airport's approach and obstacle-limitation zones, so tower heights need Airport Authority of India / Indian Air Force clearance in addition to standard GMADA building norms.
